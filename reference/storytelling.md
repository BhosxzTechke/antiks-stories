# Storytelling — Cold Open + Story Payoff

## Structure (62–75 s)
| Time | Beat | Rules |
|---|---|---|
| 0–2 s | **Cold open** | The most shocking/mysterious moment FIRST. Text hook on screen from frame 1 (sound-off viewers). No logo, no "hi". |
| 2–5 s | **Rewind** | "But to understand how... we go back to 1944." Use `Antiks.fx.rewind` + rewind.wav. |
| 5–50 s | **Story** | Cause → stakes → escalation. A **mini-hook every 10–15 s** ("But that wasn't the strangest part."). Each mini-hook pays off within ~10 s — no stacking unresolved loops. |
| 50–65 s | **Payoff** | Reveal the **WHY / HOW**. The cold open showed only the WHAT — never spend the payoff in the hook. |
| 65–72 s | **Ending** | Final line connects to frame 1 (loop). Then one specific question ("Would you have surrendered?"), never "comment below". |

## Choosing the opening style (pick from the story's strongest asset)
| Strongest asset | Opening style | Example shape |
|---|---|---|
| A strange or striking image | Unexpected visual opening | Silent strange scene + one line of text |
| An impossible-sounding number/fact | Contradiction / unbelievable fact | "The war ended in 1945. He kept fighting for 29 years." |
| A person in danger or at a decision | POV / in medias res | "1899. A 24-year-old general. 60 men. One mountain pass." |
| Something unsolved | Mystery / cliffhanger | "Nobody knows who sent the letter." |
| Two eras / before-after | Comparison hook | "This street was once..." |
| A common belief that's wrong | "You've been told wrong" | Only if 100% verified; glitch + record-scratch moment |

## Hook scoring (write 3, score each 1–5, recommend the highest)
- **Stop power:** would a scroller freeze in 1 s?
- **Curiosity gap:** does it create a question the viewer *needs* answered?
- **Mute clarity:** does the on-screen text alone make sense?
- **Payoff protection:** does it keep the WHY/HOW for the end?
- **Accuracy:** is every word defensible with sources?
Rotate styles: not the same style as the last 2–3 entries in `data/video-log.md`.

## Writing rules
- Spoken English, short sentences, one idea each. Numbers as words in `script.json` text only when Kokoro misreads them; captions show digits.
- Concrete over abstract: names, places, years, objects.
- Give outsiders context in ≤5 words ("in the Philippines", "Spanish-era Manila").
- Emotional truth: show the human stakes (fear, pride, loss), not just facts.
- Multi-part series (occasional, big stories only): label "Part 1 of 3" on screen; each part needs its own payoff and ends with a clear tease of the next part.
