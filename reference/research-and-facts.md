# Research, facts and archive images

## Fact rules
- Every key fact (names, dates, numbers, causes, quotes) needs **2 independent reliable sources**. Wikipedia is a map to sources, not a final source alone.
- Good sources: academic/university pages, national archives and libraries (National Historical Commission of the Philippines, National Library of the Philippines, US National Archives, Library of Congress), museums, reputable newspapers (Inquirer, Rappler, Philstar, GMA, BBC, NYT, AP), books with authors.
- Weak sources (never alone): content farms, listicles, social posts, AI-written blogs.
- **Myths and legends** are welcome content but must be labelled on screen/in narration ("legend says...", "the story goes..."). Common traps: "first in the world" claims, "Filipino invented X" (often "popularised" or "improved"), viral quotes with no source.
- Disputed facts: say so ("historians still argue about...") — honesty builds trust and comments.
- Record sources in `brief.md` (URL + what each supports). Put a short "Sources:" line in the publish pack.

## Story finder scoring (1–5 each)
Surprise · Emotion/stakes · Visual potential · Verifiability · Filipino pride/shareability. Skip anything under 3 on verifiability.

## Archive images
Use only:
- **Public domain**: US federal government works (US Army Signal Corps, National Archives, Library of Congress — many 1898–1946 Philippine photos), works whose copyright expired, items marked PD on Wikimedia Commons with a valid reason (check the file page's licence box).
- **CC0** or clearly licensed for commercial use (CC BY only if we add the required credit on screen + in description).
Avoid: newspaper/agency photos still in copyright, Getty/AP/Reuters, random Google Images, AI-generated "historical" images.
Process: download into `archive/`, add a row to the video's `licenses.csv`: `archive/file.jpg,<source page URL>,<licence>,<what it shows>`. Show a short credit under the photo ("US National Archives, 1945").
If no usable real photo exists, use a document/map/newspaper-style doodle instead — never fake a "real photo".
